# Skill: classifying study design

## Reading the design from a schematic
The study schematic answers most of this domain faster than the prose. Horizontal
bands are epochs; parallel horizontal tracks are arms; a crossing or swap between
tracks means crossover. Arrows converging on one track after divergence usually
means a common follow-up.

## Telling parallel from crossover
| Signal | Reading |
|---|---|
| 'randomised to receive A or B' | parallel |
| 'randomised to one of two sequences' | crossover |
| 'washout period' | crossover, almost always |
| 'Period 1 / Period 2' with different treatments | crossover |
| SoA columns repeating per period | crossover |
| single arm, no comparator | single group |

A crossover misclassified as parallel produces the wrong number of study cells
and a schedule that cannot be built, so this distinction is worth spending
attention on.

## Blinding
`Open Label` is an answer. Record it. Where the protocol describes asymmetric
blinding -- participants unblinded, assessors blinded -- that is single blind
with the specifics in the description. Where a study is described as
'assessor-blinded', do not upgrade it to double blind.

## Phase
Phase appears on the title page far more often than in the body. Japanese and EU
protocols sometimes write 'Phase III' where the codelist says 'Phase III Trial' --
match on meaning.

## Intent versus sub-type
Intent is the clinical purpose: treatment, prevention, diagnosis, supportive
care. Sub-type is the investigation kind: efficacy, safety, pharmacokinetic,
bioequivalence. A study normally has one intent and several sub-types, and a
pharmacokinetic sub-type coexists happily with a treatment intent.
