# Skill: objectives and endpoints

## The pairing is the point
Objectives and endpoints are almost always printed as a two-column table, and
that pairing is what a statistical analysis plan consumes. Preserve it exactly.
Never regroup an endpoint under a different objective because it seems to fit
better.

## Synopsis versus body
The synopsis table is the cleanest source of the pairing. The body section is
more likely to be current after an amendment. Read both; prefer the body where
they conflict and record the conflict as a gap -- an un-amended synopsis is a
real and common protocol defect worth surfacing.

## Levels
Primary, secondary, exploratory. Where the table groups rows under a level
heading, every row inherits it. Where no levels are given at all, do not assume
the first is primary unless the text supports it.

## Verbatim text
Keep the clinical content:

> "To demonstrate that nasal glucagon is non-inferior to intramuscular glucagon
> in the treatment of insulin-induced hypoglycaemia"

Not 'efficacy'. The whole value of the extracted objective is in the detail that
a summary discards.

## One endpoint or several
- 'Change in glucose at 15, 30 and 45 minutes' -- one endpoint, three time points
- 'Incidence of adverse events and change in glucose' -- two endpoints
- 'Time to glucose recovery, defined as ...' -- one endpoint with its definition

When splitting is genuinely ambiguous, keep the protocol's own row structure.
